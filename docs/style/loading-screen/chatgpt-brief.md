## Prompt

Create a new loading-screen illustration for my WW2 grand-strategy board game, *Quartermaster General*. It's a 16:9 full-screen image at 2560×1440. The game's UI uses a "brass on olive" design system: dark olive-black glass panels, cream text and antique brass as the single accent colour. The loading screen should look like it belongs to that system. Image 1 shows the system, images 2–3 show the menu and in-game screens that come before and after this one, and image 4 is the current loading screen I want to replace.

**Subject and composition**
- Keep the theme of the current image: the logistics of a world war. Supply depots, trains, ships, tanks, aircraft, troops, and commanders studying maps around a map of Europe and the world.
- Use a framed composition. Put the dense hero art (a commander portrait, armour, aircraft, smoke) in the left and right thirds. Keep the centre calm and low in detail (sky, haze or a faint map) so it reads as a resting space.
- The bottom ~12% of the image fades to near-black (#050604). A status line ("Loading…") gets drawn there in-game, so keep that strip quiet and free of figures or bright detail.
- No text, logos, lettering, UI elements, borders or frames in the image. The game adds all of those.

**Colour (match the design system)**
- Grade the whole image warm sepia-olive: deep olive-black shadows (#0C0D0A to #1B170E), khaki and olive mid-tones, and brass/gold highlights (#D9AD5C, #EDC26E) where light catches metal, smoke and sky. The brightest highlights are a pale warm cream (#FFEDBD / #EDE5D1), never pure white.
- Use brass as the main light accent: a low sun, firelight or reflections glinting off helmets, gun barrels and rails. Keep saturated colours to small touches (flags, fire). Avoid blues and teals that pull away from the olive-and-brass palette. The current image is slightly too grey-blue in the sky and sea.
- Add a strong vignette into near-black at all edges, so a dark olive-glass panel or brass-trimmed UI can sit over any edge without clashing.

**Style**
- Painterly, cinematic digital illustration like the main-menu art: heroic 1940s war-poster energy and rich detail in the figures, with fine film grain and a subtle aged-paper or canvas texture over the whole image.
- Use dramatic side and rim lighting with high contrast between dark olive shadow and brass light.
- Keep it moody and premium rather than busy. It should feel like a dark brass-framed war room.

**Avoid**: text or pseudo-letters, watermarks, modern equipment, bright blue skies, neon or pure white highlights, cluttering the centre or the bottom strip, and visible borders or frames.
